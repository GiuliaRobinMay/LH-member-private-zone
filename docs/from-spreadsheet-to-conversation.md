# From a spreadsheet to a conversation

What happens to the call sheet, column by column. Nothing is lost — the same research, presented
the way a person actually uses it while holding a phone.

## Before

The AI Grant Researcher returns two separate things:

1. A **14-column spreadsheet**, one row per organisation.
2. A **narrative "Action Plan"** in a different file, which is where the genuinely useful advice
   lives — *"your absolute first call should be to the Idaho SBDC at 208-426-3875"*.

The member gets a grid. The advice sits somewhere else. Nothing is saved.

## After

One document, in this order:

1. **What you told us** — the member's own words, quoted back.
2. **The opening** — what was found and what it means, in plain language.
3. **Before you start** — a gold callout: what to have ready.
4. **Watch out** — a red callout, where there's a scam or a dead programme to avoid.
5. **Start with this one call** — the Action Plan's first recommendation, promoted to a hero card
   with a big tappable number and the words to say.
6. **Your plan** — the rest of the Action Plan as numbered steps.
7. **Then make these calls** — every other organisation as an expandable card with a tick box.

## Column mapping

| Original column | Where it lives now |
|---|---|
| Name of Organization | Card heading |
| Website (full URL) | **Their website** button |
| Contact Email Address | **Email them** button |
| Contact Phone Number | Big green tappable `tel:` pill — the single most important element on a phone |
| Contact Information (address) | Small print at the foot of the expanded card |
| Eligibility Requirements | *Who this is for* |
| Area Requirements | *Where they help* |
| Dates of Eligibility | *When to apply* |
| Description of Services Available | Rewritten as **plain-language "what they do"**, shown on the collapsed card — the one line a member reads before deciding to call |
| Dollar Value of Free Services | *What you get free* |
| Grants or Loans Available | *Kind of help* |
| Max Amount of Grants or Loans | Chip on the collapsed card + *How much* |
| Important Things to Consider Before Applying | Blue **"Before you call"** callout |
| 30 Second Call Script | **"What to say when they answer"** — gold-edged box with a **Copy these words** button |
| *(new)* | **Called** tick box, saved between visits |

The spreadsheet export keeps all 14 columns in their original order and adds the *Called* column, so
anyone who already works from the spreadsheet loses nothing.

## Why the shape changed

- **The phone number is the point.** It is the largest, most tappable thing on every card. Everything
  else supports the moment the member actually dials.
- **One decision at a time.** A 14-column grid asks a nervous person to compare ten organisations at
  once. A hero "start with this one call" asks them to do one thing.
- **The script has to be readable out loud.** It is set larger than body text, in quotation marks,
  with a copy button — because members read it while the phone is ringing.
- **Progress is visible.** Tick boxes are already how Lesko print guides work. Here they persist
  between visits.
- **Plain words.** "Non-dilutive capital" became "money you don't pay back". The research is the same;
  the reading level is not.
